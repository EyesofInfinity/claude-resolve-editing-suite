# Documentary and true story

Real people and real events, shaped into a story. The film finds meaning without inventing it. Patience and truth are the craft.

## Starting points [default]

| Decision | Start with |
|---|---|
| Length | What the story holds. A few minutes to a feature |
| Shot length | 3 to 10 seconds for observational shots, far longer for a moment that plays |
| Narration | Prefer the subjects' own voices. Add narration only for what they cannot say |
| Grade | Honest and consistent. Differences between places may stay |
| Music | Less than feels natural at first. Silence and real sound carry weight |

## Find the story in the material

1. Log everything: text descriptions of each clip, transcripts of all speech. Reason over the log.
2. Write the story on paper from what actually exists, as a list of scenes.
3. Find a character who wants something, an obstacle and a question that pulls forward.
4. Build scenes with a beginning, turn and end. Each should change something.
5. Cut anything that repeats. A beautiful scene that does not move the story goes.

## Cutting

- Let scenes play. Hold for reactions and for things that happen in real time.
- Come in late and leave early, within what is true.
- Alternate density: an interview-driven passage, then a scene with no talk. Quiet passages let the last idea land.
- Cut on looks and on actions. People watching things are natural cut points.
- Use L- and J-cuts constantly. A voice carrying over a place is the form's basic tool.
- Long, lightly cut films can hold a large audience when something unresolved keeps pulling. [practice]

## Ethics

- Do not change the meaning of what someone said. Do not place words or images so they imply something false.
- Label reconstructions and generated images clearly, or do not use them.
- Check facts and names. Confirm rights for archive and music.
- Consider the person on screen when choosing what to show.

## Picture

- Establish a place before living in it, or choose a good reason to withhold it.
- Details say what wide shots cannot: hands, objects, signs.
- Archive material: keep its look, frame it as it was shot, and credit it. Public archives are a useful source, and rights need checking per clip.
- A slow move on a photograph toward what matters. See [picture.md](../picture.md).

## Sound

Real sound first. Ambience under everything. A location's sound starts before its picture. Bring music in late and take it out early. See [sound.md](../sound.md).

## Common failures

- Interview after interview with B-roll on top.
- Constant music.
- Story told in narration while the picture decorates it.
- No question pulling the viewer forward.

## Ask the user

What happened, and why does it matter now? Who is the main person? What footage, archive and access exists?
