# Explainer

Narration and designed visuals make an idea clear. The viewer leaves feeling smarter. The picture does real explaining, and the facts must hold up.

## Starting points [default]

| Decision | Start with |
|---|---|
| Length | 45 seconds to 3 minutes for a single idea. Longer needs more turns |
| Script speed | 130 to 150 words a minute. 90 to 110 words for 45 seconds |
| Visual change | Something new every 2 to 4 seconds |
| Clock | The voice. Picture changes on the words. Music under at 10 LU or more below |
| Look | One clear style. See below |

## Order

1. Open with a question or a surprising fact, with no logo and no product.
2. Build the answer one part at a time.
3. A turn: the thing the viewer did not expect.
4. The answer, in one image they can recall.
5. If it sells something, the product comes last, as the natural next step.

## The facts come first

Make a claim sheet before any script: each claim, its source, its date, a visual that could show it. Use only supported claims. Check the sources yourself. A convincing animation makes a false claim more convincing. Say "a", and avoid "the first", unless sourced.

## Picture

Pick by what the idea needs:

| Idea | Style |
|---|---|
| Maths, AI, systems | One shape morphing into the next |
| Data | Axes, then data, then one label |
| Software | Real interface with big, clear states |
| History, journalism | Collage, maps, clippings, one highlight colour |
| Scale | Moving between tiny and huge |
| Relationships | A hand or pen drawing as the voice speaks |
| A story | An illustrated character and a process in order |

- One constant the eye can hold, kept on screen while the idea develops.
- Highlight only the part being discussed.
- Show it, then say it, or show it as you say it. Never read the screen aloud.
- Act abstract ideas out literally.
- Let the viewer read each label before the next appears.

## Sound

Conversational narration, dry and close. Music changes at section changes. Sparse effects that voice what happens. A drop to quiet before the key reveal. See [sound.md](../sound.md).

## Common failures

- A slide deck with a voice.
- Every point the same size on screen.
- Screens full of words.
- Facts no one checked.
- Fast narration with no space for the idea to land.

## Ask the user

What should the viewer understand at the end? Who are they? Is there a script or a source? Must it sell anything?
