# Interview, testimonial and customer story

A real person says something true. Belief is the product. Keep what makes them believable, and shape everything around their best moments.

## Starting points [default]

| Decision | Start with |
|---|---|
| Length | 60 to 180 seconds for a testimonial; longer only if the story holds |
| Cut pace | `--pace relaxed` |
| Grade | Natural skin, one look across all sittings. `Warm Interview` as a start |
| Music | A soft bed that changes at the turn, 15 LU or more under speech on quiet lines |
| B-roll | Their real place, work and hands |

## Find the story

1. Transcribe everything and read it all. Mark moments of feeling, specific detail and plain truth.
2. Build the spine from their own words: where they were, what was wrong, what changed, where they are now.
3. Open on the most specific or surprising line, even when it came late in the interview.
4. End on a line that lands, usually about the person and not about the product.

Numbers come from them, and they approve what is claimed. Use only what they said. Never alter meaning by cutting words together. Show the person the final edit when the use is commercial.

## Cutting

- Keep their rhythm. Pauses and hesitations often carry the truth.
- Cover joins with B-roll or a second angle. Choose a cut on a natural breath when there is no cover.
- Give emotional lines time, before and after. A held face after a hard sentence is the strongest shot you have.
- Use reaction and detail shots to give the eye a rest: hands, a photo, the place.
- Do not illustrate every sentence.

## Picture

- Off-centre framing with the person looking across the frame toward the interviewer's side. Direct to lens for a confession or an address. Keep the side consistent.
- Soft side light and a darker background. Real locations beat blank walls.
- Two sizes (medium and close) make cuts easy. A single camera can punch in on 4K footage.

## Sound

Speech is everything. Room tone matched across cuts. A lavalier and a boom, if both exist, each cover the other. See [sound.md](../sound.md).

## Text

Name and role once. A key number or result on screen in plain text, confirmed by the person. Captions for social.

## Common failures

- Coached lines that nobody would say.
- Music telling the viewer how to feel.
- Every hesitation cut out until it sounds fake.
- A product pitch in a customer's mouth.
- Rearranged words that change what they meant.

## Ask the user

What is true about this person's result, and what can they prove? Who approves the final? Are there other angles or pictures of the before?
